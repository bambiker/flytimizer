How far can my drone fly and still get back?

Updated 8 Oct 2026 · Flytimizer guides

Short answer: It depends on which way you fly. A drone's spec-sheet range assumes calm air and no load, but in wind the round trip costs more battery in some directions than in others, and payload, cold and an older pack shrink it further. So the real answer is not a circle on the map but a shape stretched by the wind. In Flytimizer, pick your drone, put a start point anywhere, press Calculate, and the map shows how far you can fly in every direction and still land with a 20% reserve.

Why the range isn't a circle

A multirotor uses roughly the same power at a given airspeed whatever the wind, so its battery use follows its time in the air. A headwind leg takes longer than a tailwind leg saves (see how wind affects battery life), so any round trip in wind costs more than the same trip in calm air. How much more depends on the direction:

  • Along the wind (into it and back, or with it and back) the drone spends a long time in the headwind on one of the legs.
  • Across the wind it has to angle into the wind both ways, which slows it a little each way, but it never faces the full headwind.
  • With a payload one way (a delivery), it matters which leg is heavy: carrying the load with the wind behind you and coming back light into the wind reaches further than the other way round.

On top of that, the cold reduces how much of the battery's energy you can use, and a heavier drone uses more power every minute.

An example

A DJI Mavic 3 Classic (77 Wh battery, 46 minutes rated flight time) flying at its full 21 m/s, out and back from one point, landing at the far end for 30 seconds, keeping a 20% reserve. The wind blows from the north at about 7 m/s at 30 m and 9 m/s at 80 m. These are Flytimizer's estimates, good to about ±20%:

A delivery carried into the wind reaches about 45% less far than in calm air with no load, and about 20% less far than the same delivery carried downwind. If your drop-off point is 10 km to the north, it's out of reach today with a safe reserve, even though it would be fine in calm air, or 10 km to the south.

Why it matters

  • Know before you take off. Finding out halfway that the battery won't make it back, into a headwind that has picked up, is how drones end up in forced landings. A range map tells you in advance whether the place you want to reach is safely within range today.
  • Choose where to launch from. Moving the launch point upwind, so that the heavy or long leg flies with the wind, can bring a target into range.
  • Choose when to fly. The wind changes by the hour. Flytimizer's 48-hour outlook lets you pick an hour and see the range for that time.
  • Plan deliveries and inspections. For a service area, it shows at a glance which addresses you can reach with a given drone and payload, and which need a closer launch point or a bigger drone.

What the range doesn't include

The outline is a straight line out and back at your drone's set speed. It doesn't include detours around terrain, buildings or restricted areas, which need a real route (set a destination for that). And it shows what the battery allows, not what the law allows: most countries require you to keep the drone in sight unless you have a specific authorization, and that limit is usually much shorter.

FAQ

How far can a drone fly and come back?

Roughly half the distance it could fly one way on a full battery, minus a reserve, but wind, payload, cold and battery age all reduce it, and in wind it differs by direction. For example, Flytimizer estimates about 16.6 km out and back for a DJI Mavic 3 Classic in calm air at full speed with a 20% reserve, but about 9 km into a moderate wind when carrying a 30% heavier load out. Check the range for your drone, place and time before flying.

Does wind reduce a drone's range?

Yes. A round trip in wind always uses more battery than in calm air, because the headwind leg costs more than the tailwind leg saves. With the same load both ways, trips along the wind lose more range than trips across it; with a heavier load one way, flying that leg with the wind behind you reaches further.

Does cold weather reduce drone range?

Yes. Lithium batteries deliver less of their energy when cold, about 15–20% less around 0 °C, so the range shrinks by about as much. Keep batteries warm before flight.

Is my drone's maximum range the legal limit?

No. The range is what the battery allows. Most countries require the drone to stay within your visual line of sight unless you have a specific authorization, which is usually a much shorter distance. Check your local rules.
